## 1. Executive Summary

The 42-rai property splits into two purposes with two very different financial jobs:

- **10 rai — Angel Arms Horse Sanctuary.** Owned and run by **The Angel Arms Foundation** (an existing foundation, currently serving special-needs children and struggling families). **This is a paid retirement/boarding facility, not an adoption or rescue program** — horse owners keep ownership of their horse and pay the Foundation **฿15,000/month plus veterinary fees** for full-service care. The facility also runs an equine-assisted therapy program for special-needs children, using well-mannered resident horses (with their owners' consent). **This is the only piece that needs your own capital — and, unlike a donation-funded rescue, it is a real paying business.**
- **32 rai — a subdivided equestrian residential development, sold off-plan under a partnership with the land owner.** Split into 15–18 plots (1–5 rai each) and sold to horse-owning families at ฿3.5M/rai, with 5 rai kept as shared communal space. Under the deal with the owner, **the owner keeps the deed and transfers title directly to each end-buyer, in step with the ฿73.6M payment plan** — so that land cost is vendor-financed against incoming plot sales, not capital you front. **Your own boarding clients are a primary target market for these plots** — a family that already boards a horse with you and likes the community is the most natural buyer for a plot next door.

**The real capital ask is the sanctuary itself: ~฿54.7M** (฿23M land + ฿31.7M construction/fit-out). Because boarding is a paid service rather than a donation-dependent rescue, **the sanctuary is projected to be cash-flow positive from Year 1** — a materially stronger position than a typical charity rescue.

**Key numbers:**
- Sanctuary land payments (yours): ฿23M, **Oct 2026 deposit + Apr 2027 balance**
- 32-rai land payments (owner-financed, pass-through against plot sales): ฿73.6M, quarterly Jul 2027 → Oct 2028 — **not your capital**
- Sanctuary capital need: **~฿54.7M** (land + construction/fit-out)
- Sanctuary operating result: **positive from Year 1** (~฿320k net), growing to **~฿4.47M by Year 5**, on ~฿15,000/month/horse boarding fees (vet fees billed and paid directly by owners, pass-through)
- Combined (sanctuary + community amenities) net: **~฿342k in Year 1, growing to ~฿5.6M by Year 5**
- Horses in residence: 15 (Year 1) → 35 (Year 5). Children served in therapy: 120 → 500/year.

## 2. Vision & Mission

**Vision:** to be the leading horse retirement, wellness, and equine-therapy facility in the Khao Yai–Nakhon Nayok region, anchoring a resident community of horse-owning families — and a flagship program of The Angel Arms Foundation.

**Mission:**
- Provide dedicated, full-service **retirement and wellness boarding** for horses, cared for under Foundation standards, at ฿15,000/month plus vet fees paid directly by the owner.
- Run an **equine-assisted therapy program** for the special-needs children and families the Foundation already serves, using calm, well-trained resident horses (with owner consent — see §8).
- Build a resident community of horse-owning families, drawn first from boarding clients, whose land purchase funds itself.
- Offer education, training, and career opportunities to young people and the community through the sanctuary's operations.

**Core values:** Horse welfare and owner trust, children/family welfare, financial sustainability, community, environmental stewardship of the land.

## 3. Business Concept & Structure

Three linked pieces, each with a distinct financial job:

1. **The Angel Arms Foundation (existing)** — owns the 10-rai sanctuary and its mission: paid horse retirement boarding, the children's equine-therapy program, donor and grant relationships for the therapy side specifically. No new charity registration needed.
2. **A land-development/sales entity, operating as the owner's partner** — the original land owner retains legal title to the 32 rai and transfers the deed directly to each end-buyer, in step with the owner's own ฿73.6M payment plan. This entity subdivides, markets, and sells the 15–18 plots — **drawing its first and best leads from the sanctuary's own boarding clients** — funds the subdivision infrastructure, passes the owner's share through, and keeps the margin.
3. **A community-amenities operating company** — runs the ongoing café, shared glamping/EV camping/floating villa, and collects a common-area (HOA-style) maintenance fee from plot owners.

**Confirm with counsel:** the exact legal form of the owner partnership, how the Foundation's land (10 rai) is legally separated from the 32 rai still held by the owner, and the tax treatment of the resale.

## 7. Market Analysis

**Demand drivers:**
- Thai horse owners currently have few dedicated, professionally run **retirement/wellness boarding** options for aging or retired horses — most livery is geared to active competition horses, not long-term retirement care. A facility built specifically for this is a clear gap.
- A dedicated equine-therapy program is a strong differentiator versus generic boarding stables, and a meaningful CSR/grant story for the Foundation.
- There's real demand from Thai horse-owning families for land near an established equestrian facility.

**Target customers, in priority order:**
1. **Horse owners seeking retirement/wellness boarding** — the sanctuary's core paying customer, and the primary lead source for plot sales (see below).
2. **Boarding clients as plot buyers** — once a family boards a horse here, they are already invested in the community; converting them into 32-rai plot owners is the single highest-probability sales motion available, and should be treated as the default sales funnel rather than a side benefit.
3. Corporate CSR partners and grant-making foundations (funding the therapy program).
4. Schools and hospitals referring families for equine-assisted therapy.
5. Existing Foundation donors.
6. Farm De Lek's existing visitor base.

**Competition:** other Thai equestrian boarding facilities compete for boarding clients on price and quality of care; other rural land subdivisions compete for plot buyers. The sanctuary's combination of professional retirement care plus an on-site community is the differentiator against both.

## 8. Operations Plan

**Horse intake (boarding, not rescue):** a prospective boarder's horse undergoes a veterinary and temperament assessment, then a short settling-in/quarantine period before joining general turnout — standard biosecurity practice for any multi-horse facility, not a rescue rehabilitation process. **Vet fees are billed directly to the owner** (the Foundation coordinates but does not mark up or absorb this cost).

**Therapy program:** run under Foundation staff/protocols, using resident boarded horses whose owners consent to therapy participation — typically incentivized with a modest boarding-fee discount, since a calm, well-trained retiree is often better suited to therapy work than a horse in active competition training. A certified equine-therapy specialist screens each horse for suitability.

**Sanctuary staffing (Year 1, ~7 people, headcount scaling with horse count):**

| Role | Count |
|---|---|
| Sanctuary manager | 1 |
| Grooms/horse handlers | 3 (→ more as horse count grows toward 35) |
| Veterinarian (contract/on-call, coordinating owner-billed care) | 1 |
| Equine-therapy specialist | 1 |
| Admin (billing, boarding contracts, bookings) | 1 |

**Community amenities carries its own small hospitality/upkeep crew (~3 people)** — separate budget, see §11.4.

**Animal welfare compliance:** register/operate under Thailand's Animal Welfare Act, and adopt clear facility care standards — this matters for boarding-client trust as much as for the therapy program's credibility.

## 9. Marketing Strategy

1. **Sell retirement boarding as the core service** — target Thai horse owners (via equestrian clubs, riding schools, vets) who need dedicated retirement/wellness care, not just active-competition livery.
2. **Treat every boarding client as a plot-sale lead** — build the sales process so that boarding clients get a first look/priority reservation on 32-rai plots before general marketing. This is the top-priority sales motion, not an afterthought.
3. **Leverage the existing Angel Arms Foundation brand and donor base** for the therapy program and community goodwill.
4. **Farm De Lek synergy** for modest visitor traffic and cross-promotion.
5. **CSR/grant outreach** for the therapy program specifically.
6. **School/hospital referral network** for therapy referrals.
7. **Resident-community word-of-mouth** once early plots sell.

## 12. Risk Analysis & Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Boarding occupancy below the 15→35 horse plan | Price and market boarding competitively against the retirement-care gap identified in §7; the plan's operating cost lines scale with actual horse count, limiting downside |
| Plot sales lag the owner's ฿73.6M milestones | Get shortfall terms explicit in the partnership contract; the boarding-client sales funnel (§7, §9) reduces this risk versus cold marketing |
| Subdivision infrastructure fronted before deposits arrive | Sequence roads/utilities first; phase shared amenities after an initial sales tranche |
| SBT/resale tax worse than estimated, or falls on you | Confirm seller-of-record status and exact tax exposure with an accountant |
| Vet-fee billing disputes with owners | Standard boarding agreement (§10) should specify the vet coordination/billing process clearly up front |
| Horse disease outbreak | Mandatory quarantine/settling-in protocol, contracted vet, biosecurity procedures |
| Flood damage | Confirm flood-elevation data before finalizing floating-villa/pasture placement |
| Therapy session incident | Certified specialist, strict horse-suitability screening, liability insurance |
| Land title/subdivision issues | Complete formal legal re-survey before signing |
