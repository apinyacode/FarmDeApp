# Business Plan: Farm De Rai — Rent-to-Buy Tourism & EV Hub
### A pilot path to buying the 42-rai Nakhon Nayok property — and gifting 12 rai to the horse sanctuary

**Location:** Nakhon Nayok Province, Thailand (adjacent to Farm De Lek)
**Land:** 42 rai — **leased in full**, not purchased upfront
**Model:** Lease the whole property for 5 years, build a farm-stay (annual rental) + EV-charging + horse retirement stable business on it, then at Year 5 sell off the relocatable farm-stay units and buy the land outright, partnering with the owner throughout
**Prepared for:** Apinya
**Date:** September 2026

> **How this connects to the horse sanctuary plan.** This is not a competing, unrelated idea to `HORSE_SANCTUARY_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` — it's a different *path* to the same land, chosen when buying the full 42 rai upfront isn't yet fundable or de-risked enough. The core idea: **rent the land first, run a real business on it, and use that operating track record to test the concept and build the commitment (from partners, lenders, and the owner) needed to eventually buy the whole 42 rai.** Once the purchase closes at (or before) Year 5, **12 of the 42 rai are gifted to the horse sanctuary**, and the remaining **30 rai stay with the tourism business**. In other words: this plan is the funding and proof-of-concept engine that gets the sanctuary its land without requiring ~฿96.6M in land capital on day one. All figures below are planning-level estimates — verify land rent terms, construction/procurement quotes, and the purchase-option terms with the owner before committing capital.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Instead of buying any of the 42 rai upfront, this plan **leases the whole property from the owner for ฿4,000/rai/year (~฿168,000/year total)**, and puts capital into **relocatable, fast-to-deploy assets** rather than land: 20 pre-built farm-stay units, a 10-bay EV park-and-stay ground with 5 DC fast chargers, and a horse retirement/boarding stable for up to 20 horses. The appeal is low upfront capital and fast time-to-revenue — you're testing and building the business on someone else's land first.

**The farm-stay units are rented by the year, not the night.** This isn't a hotel — it's a deliberate choice to sign up committed families early (occupied units ramp from 6 of 20 in Year 1 to full occupancy by Year 5) so the lease period genuinely tests demand and builds commitment toward the eventual land purchase, rather than chasing tourist occupancy rates.

**The deal structure is a genuine partnership with the owner:** they earn steady rental income for the full term, and you (or an aligned buyer) are positioned as the ready buyer to close on the land — either during the term if the business outperforms, or at the natural 5-year mark once the operating history de-risks the purchase for financing.

**At the end of Year 5**, the plan is to **sell off the 20 pre-built farm-stay units** (they're built to be relocatable, so they retain resale value) and **buy the land outright**, using five years of accumulated operating profit to fund as much of the purchase as possible. **12 of the 42 rai are then gifted to the horse sanctuary; the remaining 30 rai stay with the tourism business.**

**Key numbers:**
- Upfront capital: **~฿46.0M** (pre-built units, EV/charging infrastructure, horse stable barn + paddocks, site works) — no land purchase
- Annual land rent: **~฿168,000** (฿4,000/rai × 42 rai)
- 5-year cumulative operating profit (pure sum of 5 years' net income, before the land purchase): **~฿15.5M**
- Year 5 terminal transaction: **+฿4.0M** (house resale, est.) **− ฿96.6M** (land purchase, est.) = **−฿92.6M**
- Net cumulative cash position after buying the land (i.e. ฿15.5M operating profit − ฿46.0M upfront capex − ฿92.6M net terminal cost): **~−฿123.1M** — meaning operations alone now cover only a small fraction of the land purchase; this is the single biggest change from the earlier (restaurant-based) version of this plan and needs a real financing answer — see the flag in Section 7
- Land split after purchase: **12 rai → horse sanctuary (gift)**, **30 rai → retained by the tourism business**

## 2. Concept

- **Years 1–5: build and run the business on leased land.** Low capital risk relative to buying 42 rai outright (~฿96.6M based on the owner's earlier-quoted pricing) — you commit ~฿38M to movable/depreciable assets instead.
- **The owner benefits immediately**, not just at a future closing: steady rental income (~฿168,000/year) from land that might otherwise sit idle, plus a credible, operating buyer building a track record on their property.
- **You retain optionality.** Because the farm-stay units are pre-built/relocatable, if the business underperforms or the land purchase doesn't close, the units can be sold or moved rather than written off as sunk construction cost — a materially different risk profile than building permanent structures on land you don't yet own.
- **The close can happen before or after the 5-year mark.** If the business outperforms and financing lines up early, nothing stops closing sooner; the 5-year point is the planned "no later than" horizon, not a hard deadline.
- **The rent phase is the test.** Every year of on-the-ground operating history — real occupancy, real revenue, real cost control — is also evidence to bring to partners, lenders, and the owner that the eventual purchase is a sound bet, rather than asking anyone to commit ~฿96.6M in land capital against a plan alone.
- **The payoff for the sanctuary.** Once the 42 rai is purchased, **12 rai are gifted outright to the horse sanctuary** (see `HORSE_SANCTUARY_BUSINESS_PLAN.md`), giving it a permanent home funded by the tourism business's own operating profit rather than by donor capital raised upfront. The remaining 30 rai stay with the tourism business that earned them.

## 3. Site Concept (42 rai, leased)

- **Farm-stay zone:** 20 pre-built units, spaced for privacy with shared walking paths and landscaping. Rented annually to families, not nightly to tourists.
- **EV hub:** 10 park-and-stay bays (overnight parking + standard charging + a place to camp/sleep in or beside the vehicle) plus 5 dedicated DC fast chargers for through-traffic top-ups, positioned near the road frontage for easy access without needing to enter the overnight-guest area.
- **Horse retirement stable:** a 20-stall barn with tack/feed room and paddock fencing, taking in horses on paid boarding (same ฿15,000/month/horse model as the horse sanctuary plan) — the tourism business's own version of the sanctuary's core service, run commercially on leased land during the test period.
- **Site infrastructure:** internal roads, water, and an electrical backbone sized for the fast-charger load (this is a real engineering item — 5 DC fast chargers is a meaningful power draw; confirm grid capacity/upgrade cost with the provincial electricity authority before finalizing the charger count).
- **Reception/admin building:** check-in, site management, staff facilities.

## 4. Revenue Model

| Stream | Basis | Year 1 | Year 5 |

| Farm-stay homes (20 units) | ฿180,000/unit/year, annual rental | ฿1,080,000 | ฿3,600,000 |

| Horse retirement boarding (≤20 horses) | ฿15,000/horse/month | ฿2,700,000 | ฿3,600,000 |
| **Total revenue** | | **฿6,481,000** | **฿15,040,200** |

**Ramp assumed:** farm-stay units occupied 6→20 (of 20) as families commit, EV bays 20%→52% occupancy, charger sessions/day 4→12 per charger, horses in care 15→20 — all planning-level estimates that should be checked against comparable properties in the Khao Yai/Nakhon Nayok corridor before relying on this plan for financing.

**Trading a hotel-scale revenue line for a lower, steadier one.** Switching the farm-stay units from nightly tourist pricing to annual family rental — and dropping the restaurant entirely — cuts total revenue by roughly 60–65% versus the tourism-heavy version of this plan. That's the deliberate trade: a smaller, more predictable business built around committed tenants and a real horse-care service, rather than a hospitality operation that needs restaurant-scale foot traffic to work. See Section 7 for what this means for the Year 5 land purchase.

| | ฿ |

| **Cumulative after buying the land** | **(123,142,800)** |

*(Note: "5-year cumulative operating profit" in Section 1 — ~฿15.5M — is the pure sum of the five years' net income. The ฿(35.4M) row above is that same figure net of the ฿46.0M Year-0 capex outflow, i.e. the actual cash position on hand right before the Year 5 buy/sell transaction — already negative before the land purchase even happens.)*

**⚠️ The headline finding, and it's a big shift from the earlier version of this plan:** with the restaurant removed and farm-stay switched to annual rental, this business no longer comes close to self-funding the Year 5 land purchase from its own operations. Five years of net income (~฿15.5M) doesn't even cover the ~฿46.0M upfront capex, let alone the ~฿96.6M land price — the model shows a cumulative cash position of **~−฿123.1M** after buying the land, roughly **three times worse** than the ~−฿41.0M shown in the restaurant-based version. **This plan, as scoped, cannot buy the land from operating cash flow alone.** It needs one or more of:
1. **External financing at Year 5** (bank loan, investor equity, or a phased/partial purchase) covering the bulk of the ~฿96.6M+ shortfall — this is now the central financing question, not a footnote.
2. **Higher farm-stay rent or faster occupancy ramp** than the ~฿180,000/unit/year, 6→20 units assumed here — worth testing against real demand from interested families rather than treating this as fixed.
3. **A different Year 5 exit** — e.g., only a partial land purchase, or extending the lease/negotiating a lower price given the owner's years of reliable rental income and a demonstrated operating track record.

The "test and build commitment" logic still holds — the point of this phase is to prove real demand at these lower, more conservative revenue assumptions and negotiate a workable Year 5 deal from there — but the plan should not be read as "this business pays for the land"; it should be read as "this business proves the concept and buys time to arrange financing for the land."

**After the purchase closes (however it's financed), the 42 rai splits 12 / 30:** 12 rai are gifted to the horse sanctuary (its permanent home), and 30 rai are retained by the tourism business.

**Two levers that materially change this outcome, worth negotiating explicitly:**
1. **Rent credit toward purchase.** A true "rent-to-own" deal often credits some or all of the ~฿840,000 paid in rent over 5 years against the purchase price. It's a small number here relative to the land price, but it's a real negotiating point with the owner and costs them little to grant.
2. **The actual negotiated land price at Year 5.** This plan reuses the ~฿2.3M/rai figure from earlier discussions as a placeholder. A partnership-structured deal (owner gets years of reliable rental income, plus a buyer with a demonstrated operating track record) is a reasonable basis to negotiate a better price or terms than a cold market sale — worth raising directly.

## 8. Risk Analysis

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Financing the ~฿123M shortfall at Year 5 doesn't come together** (the single biggest risk in this version of the plan) | Start structuring this financing (bank loan, investor equity, or a phased/partial land purchase) in Year 1–2, not as a Year-5 scramble; treat the Year 5 purchase as financing-dependent, not self-funded |
| Annual farm-stay rent (฿180,000/unit/year) or occupancy ramp (6→20 units) proves too optimistic — or too conservative | Test actual demand from interested families early (waitlist, deposits, LOIs) to calibrate the real rent level and ramp before relying on this number for financing conversations |
| Horse boarding demand doesn't reach 15–20 horses, or churns | Cross-market with the horse sanctuary's own network of horse-owning families — the two plans should be feeding each other leads, not competing for the same horse owners |
| Grid capacity insufficient for 5 DC fast chargers | Confirm available capacity and upgrade cost with the provincial electricity authority before finalizing charger count/spec |
| Owner doesn't grant a firm purchase option | Get the purchase-option terms (price basis, rent-credit if any, exercise window) in writing at lease signing — a lease with no purchase right is a very different, weaker plan |
| Land price rises or negotiation stalls by Year 5 | Get the price (or a pricing formula/cap) fixed in the purchase-option agreement now, not left open to renegotiate in 5 years |
| Pre-built units' resale value assumption (40%) is optimistic or pessimistic | Get a real quote from the prefab unit manufacturer on buy-back or resale terms before relying on this number |
| Horse stable barn capex (~฿13.0M) comes in over budget | Get a real quote from a stable/barn builder before relying on the ฿11.0M barn figure — it's the single largest capex line after site infrastructure |
| The 12-rai sanctuary gift isn't legally clean (title split, zoning, access) | Document the intended 12/30 rai split and the gift mechanism (subdivision + transfer, or a separate title from the start) with a lawyer before the Year 5 purchase closes, not after |

## 9. Assumptions to Verify

1. **Rent basis** — confirm ฿4,000/rai is an *annual* rate, not monthly (this plan assumes annual; if it's monthly, the rent cost is ~12× higher, though still small relative to the rest of the plan).
2. **Purchase-option terms** with the owner — price basis, any rent credit, and the exercise window (before/after 5 years, per your note).
3. **Farm-stay annual rent level and occupancy ramp** — ฿180,000/unit/year and 6→20 units are planning placeholders; test against real interest from families before relying on them.
4. **Prefab unit cost and resale value** — get real quotes from a modular/prefab builder for both the ฿500,000/unit build cost and a realistic resale/buy-back value at Year 5.
5. **Horse stable barn cost** — get a real quote for the 20-stall barn + tack/feed room (currently ฿11.0M) and the paddock fencing (฿1.5M) from a stable builder.
6. **Fast-charger economics** — confirm equipment + installation cost (highly dependent on power rating and grid connection), and realistic utilization for this specific location.
7. **Grid capacity** for 5 DC fast chargers plus the rest of the site's electrical load.
8. **Financing plan for the ~฿123M gap** at the Year 5 land purchase — this is now the plan's central open question, not a minor gap to close.
9. **Mechanism for the 12-rai sanctuary gift** — whether it's a subdivision-and-transfer after purchase, a separate title carved out at closing, or another structure; confirm with a lawyer alongside the purchase-option terms.
