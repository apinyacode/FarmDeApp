# Business Plan: Farm De Rai — Rent-to-Buy Tourism & EV Hub
### A pilot path to buying the 42-rai Nakhon Nayok property — and gifting 12 rai to the horse sanctuary

**Location:** Nakhon Nayok Province, Thailand (adjacent to Farm De Lek)
**Land:** 42 rai — **leased in full**, not purchased upfront
**Model:** Lease the whole property for 5 years, build a farm-stay + EV-charging + restaurant tourism business on it, then at Year 5 sell off the relocatable farm-stay units and buy the land outright, partnering with the owner throughout
**Prepared for:** Apinya
**Date:** September 2026

> **How this connects to the horse sanctuary plan.** This is not a competing, unrelated idea to `HORSE_SANCTUARY_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` — it's a different *path* to the same land, chosen when buying the full 42 rai upfront isn't yet fundable or de-risked enough. The core idea: **rent the land first, run a real business on it, and use that operating track record to test the concept and build the commitment (from partners, lenders, and the owner) needed to eventually buy the whole 42 rai.** Once the purchase closes at (or before) Year 5, **12 of the 42 rai are gifted to the horse sanctuary**, and the remaining **30 rai stay with the tourism business**. In other words: this plan is the funding and proof-of-concept engine that gets the sanctuary its land without requiring ~฿96.6M in land capital on day one. All figures below are planning-level estimates — verify land rent terms, construction/procurement quotes, and the purchase-option terms with the owner before committing capital.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Instead of buying any of the 42 rai upfront, this plan **leases the whole property from the owner for ฿4,000/rai/year (~฿168,000/year total)**, and puts capital into **relocatable, fast-to-deploy assets** rather than land: 20 pre-built farm-stay units, a 10-bay EV park-and-stay ground with 5 DC fast chargers, and a restaurant. The appeal is low upfront capital and fast time-to-revenue — you're testing and building the business on someone else's land first.

**The deal structure is a genuine partnership with the owner:** they earn steady rental income for the full term, and you (or an aligned buyer) are positioned as the ready buyer to close on the land — either during the term if the business outperforms, or at the natural 5-year mark once the operating history de-risks the purchase for financing.

**At the end of Year 5**, the plan is to **sell off the 20 pre-built farm-stay units** (they're built to be relocatable, so they retain resale value) and **buy the land outright**, using five years of accumulated operating profit to fund as much of the purchase as possible. **12 of the 42 rai are then gifted to the horse sanctuary; the remaining 30 rai stay with the tourism business.**

**Key numbers:**
- Upfront capital: **~฿38.0M** (pre-built units, EV/charging infrastructure, restaurant, site works) — no land purchase
- Annual land rent: **~฿168,000** (฿4,000/rai × 42 rai)
- 5-year cumulative operating profit (pure sum of 5 years' net income, before the land purchase): **~฿89.6M**
- Year 5 terminal transaction: **+฿4.0M** (house resale, est.) **− ฿96.6M** (land purchase, est.) = **−฿92.6M**
- Net cumulative cash position after buying the land (i.e. ฿89.6M operating profit − ฿38.0M upfront capex − ฿92.6M net terminal cost): **~−฿41.0M** — meaning the 5 years of operations fund the great majority of the land purchase; the rest needs financing or equity at that point
- Land split after purchase: **12 rai → horse sanctuary (gift)**, **30 rai → retained by the tourism business**

## 2. Concept

- **Years 1–5: build and run the business on leased land.** Low capital risk relative to buying 42 rai outright (~฿96.6M based on the owner's earlier-quoted pricing) — you commit ~฿38M to movable/depreciable assets instead.
- **The owner benefits immediately**, not just at a future closing: steady rental income (~฿168,000/year) from land that might otherwise sit idle, plus a credible, operating buyer building a track record on their property.
- **You retain optionality.** Because the farm-stay units are pre-built/relocatable, if the business underperforms or the land purchase doesn't close, the units can be sold or moved rather than written off as sunk construction cost — a materially different risk profile than building permanent structures on land you don't yet own.
- **The close can happen before or after the 5-year mark.** If the business outperforms and financing lines up early, nothing stops closing sooner; the 5-year point is the planned "no later than" horizon, not a hard deadline.
- **The rent phase is the test.** Every year of on-the-ground operating history — real occupancy, real revenue, real cost control — is also evidence to bring to partners, lenders, and the owner that the eventual purchase is a sound bet, rather than asking anyone to commit ~฿96.6M in land capital against a plan alone.
- **The payoff for the sanctuary.** Once the 42 rai is purchased, **12 rai are gifted outright to the horse sanctuary** (see `HORSE_SANCTUARY_BUSINESS_PLAN.md`), giving it a permanent home funded by the tourism business's own operating profit rather than by donor capital raised upfront. The remaining 30 rai stay with the tourism business that earned them.

## 3. Site Concept (42 rai, leased)

- **Farm-stay zone:** 20 pre-built units, spaced for privacy with shared walking paths and landscaping.
- **EV hub:** 10 park-and-stay bays (overnight parking + standard charging + a place to camp/sleep in or beside the vehicle) plus 5 dedicated DC fast chargers for through-traffic top-ups, positioned near the road frontage for easy access without needing to enter the overnight-guest area.
- **Restaurant:** serves farm-stay guests, EV-charging customers waiting on a fast charge, and destination day-trippers — the anchor that ties the site together and the largest single revenue line.
- **Site infrastructure:** internal roads, water, and an electrical backbone sized for the fast-charger load (this is a real engineering item — 5 DC fast chargers is a meaningful power draw; confirm grid capacity/upgrade cost with the provincial electricity authority before finalizing the charger count).
- **Reception/admin building:** check-in, site management, staff facilities.

*(Note: "5-year cumulative operating profit" in Section 1 — ~฿89.6M — is the pure sum of the five years' net income. The ฿51,600,350 row above is that same figure net of the ฿38.0M Year-0 capex outflow, i.e. the actual cash position on hand right before the Year 5 buy/sell transaction.)*

**The headline finding:** five years of operations generate enough profit to fund the great majority of the land purchase — ~฿89.6M of operating profit against a ~฿96.6M land price, though after also carrying the ฿38.0M upfront capex, the cash actually on hand at Year 5 (~฿51.6M) covers roughly half of it. The remaining ~฿41M needs financing (a mortgage against land you're about to own, a partner investment, or a portion of the accumulated cash held back rather than distributed) — this is the real number to plan around, not the land price in isolation.

**After the purchase closes, the 42 rai splits 12 / 30:** 12 rai are gifted to the horse sanctuary (its permanent home, funded this way rather than by raising land-purchase donations upfront), and 30 rai are retained by the tourism business that generated the profit funding the purchase.

**Two levers that materially change this outcome, worth negotiating explicitly:**
1. **Rent credit toward purchase.** A true "rent-to-own" deal often credits some or all of the ~฿840,000 paid in rent over 5 years against the purchase price. It's a small number here relative to the land price, but it's a real negotiating point with the owner and costs them little to grant.
2. **The actual negotiated land price at Year 5.** This plan reuses the ~฿2.3M/rai figure from earlier discussions as a placeholder. A partnership-structured deal (owner gets years of reliable rental income, plus a buyer with a demonstrated operating track record) is a reasonable basis to negotiate a better price or terms than a cold market sale — worth raising directly.

## 8. Risk Analysis

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Restaurant underperforms (it's ~47% of total revenue) | Validate concept/location with a soft-launch or pop-up before committing to the full build; keep the kitchen build modest enough to adjust the concept if needed |
| Grid capacity insufficient for 5 DC fast chargers | Confirm available capacity and upgrade cost with the provincial electricity authority before finalizing charger count/spec |
| Owner doesn't grant a firm purchase option | Get the purchase-option terms (price basis, rent-credit if any, exercise window) in writing at lease signing — a lease with no purchase right is a very different, weaker plan |
| Occupancy/utilization ramps slower than assumed | Cost lines are largely variable (COGS, some staffing) or modest fixed costs, limiting downside; land rent itself is low, so a slow start doesn't compound into a large fixed-cost burden |
| Land price rises or negotiation stalls by Year 5 | Get the price (or a pricing formula/cap) fixed in the purchase-option agreement now, not left open to renegotiate in 5 years |
| Pre-built units' resale value assumption (40%) is optimistic or pessimistic | Get a real quote from the prefab unit manufacturer on buy-back or resale terms before relying on this number |
| Financing the remaining ~฿41M at Year 5 doesn't come together | Start structuring this financing (bank loan against the now-improved land, or a phased/partial land purchase) well before Year 5, not as a Year-5 scramble |
| The 12-rai sanctuary gift isn't legally clean (title split, zoning, access) | Document the intended 12/30 rai split and the gift mechanism (subdivision + transfer, or a separate title from the start) with a lawyer before the Year 5 purchase closes, not after |

## 9. Assumptions to Verify

1. **Rent basis** — confirm ฿4,000/rai is an *annual* rate, not monthly (this plan assumes annual; if it's monthly, the rent cost is ~12× higher, though still small relative to the rest of the plan).
2. **Purchase-option terms** with the owner — price basis, any rent credit, and the exercise window (before/after 5 years, per your note).
3. **Prefab unit cost and resale value** — get real quotes from a modular/prefab builder for both the ฿500,000/unit build cost and a realistic resale/buy-back value at Year 5.
4. **Fast-charger economics** — confirm equipment + installation cost (highly dependent on power rating and grid connection), and realistic utilization for this specific location.
5. **Restaurant concept and covers/day ramp** — this is the single most important revenue assumption in the plan; validate against comparable destination restaurants in the corridor.
6. **Grid capacity** for 5 DC fast chargers plus the rest of the site's electrical load.
7. **Financing plan for the ~฿41M gap** at the Year 5 land purchase.
8. **Mechanism for the 12-rai sanctuary gift** — whether it's a subdivision-and-transfer after purchase, a separate title carved out at closing, or another structure; confirm with a lawyer alongside the purchase-option terms.
